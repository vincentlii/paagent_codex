# Breakout, Failure and Retest VPA
<!-- knowledge: chapter-001 -->

Apply only when PA identifies a breakout area, boundary test, failed breakout or retest. PA owns structure and order logic; VPA supplies additional volume-conditioned quality evidence.

A breakout is not confirmed by its breakout bar. Evaluate later closed bars: hold beyond boundary, acceptable extension, return inside and fail, or a retest that cannot regain/lose the boundary. Distinguish "no later closed response yet" from "failed"; the former is unresolved evidence, not rejection.

Read the sequence in three states:

1. Attempt: PA identifies a meaningful boundary interaction; the breakout bar is only a candidate.
2. Resolution: later closed bars either maintain progress outside, lose progress and return inside, or remain unresolved.
3. Retest: price revisits the crossed area; compare counter-move effort/result and whether the level is accepted from the new side.

A high-quality VPA contribution can be strong result with relative activity and later acceptance, or an orderly retest with reduced counter-direction result. A low-quality contribution can be high effort with little boundary progress, weak closes, immediate return, or a retest that crosses deeply back through the old structure with increasing adverse result. None of these facts overrides PA location or creates a direction.

Modest volume is not automatic rejection; it may be orderly movement, quiet timing or delayed participation. High relative activity with poor result near a boundary is a candidate for two-sided trade, absorption or exhaustion, never proven reversal. Do not call ordinary RVOL a volume climax, and do not convert a wick into a liquidity-sweep claim without order-flow data.

Avoid “liquidity sweep”; use “boundary breach and recovery candidate” absent independent liquidity data. For PA-valid retests compare effort/result with breakout leg and prior tests. Reduced activity and limited counter-trend progress can support continuation only after later response.

An upside breach that closes back inside can be an Upthrust/UT candidate only in a credible range context; a downside breach and recovery can be a Spring candidate under the symmetric conditions. Outside that context, use failed-breakout or boundary-rejection language. A failed failure, where price reclaims the breakout direction after trapping the first attempt, still requires later acceptance.

A retest can provide cleaner timing evidence for an already-valid PA direction. Describe the evidence and the missing confirmation only. Never create orders, change direction or replace PA prices with a VPA target.

## Boundary investigation without automatic failure
<!-- knowledge: chapter-002 -->

PA range or spike context can authorize investigation of boundary behavior before a breakout label is final. Read only PA-selected boundary/window facts; do not create a Setup from volume. No later response means unresolved, not rejected. Lower relative activity does not invalidate an accepted breakout; higher relative activity does not rescue a breakout that returns inside and cannot regain the boundary. Compare the crossing, later response and any retest in chronological order, keeping missing retests explicitly missing.
